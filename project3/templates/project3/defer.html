Learning to Defer

The classifier does not need to make every decision independently. A deferral strategy decides whether the AI should return its own prediction or query a simulated expert.

A useful deferral occurs when the classifier would be wrong but the selected expert is correct. The objective is therefore not simply to defer difficult examples, but to defer examples where human expertise is likely to improve the final decision.

Deferral Strategies

Confidence-Threshold Deferral

The AI defers when its largest class probability falls below a selected threshold.

This strategy is transparent and easy to interpret, but it considers only AI uncertainty. It does not explicitly model whether the expert is competent on that article.

Competence-Aware Learned Deferral

A separate binary model estimates whether querying the expert is likely to correct a classifier error.

The model uses classifier confidence, entropy, probability margin, article length, predicted class, expert region, and expected expert reliability.
